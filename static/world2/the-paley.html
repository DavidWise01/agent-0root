THE PALEY

residues that are a perfect difference set
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
The Paley construction turns the quadratic residues of a prime into a perfectly balanced combinatorial design. Take a prime p ≡ 3 (mod 4) and collect the nonzero squares mod p — the quadratic residues. This set of size (p−1)/2 is a cyclic difference set: every nonzero residue arises as a difference of two residues the same number of times, exactly (p−3)/4. Because p ≡ 3 (mod 4), −1 is a non-residue, which makes the set “skew” and gives the Paley graph and Paley’s Hadamard matrices. Structure from squaring.

LIT verified live: for every prime p ≡ 3 (mod 4) up to 59, each nonzero residue is a difference of two quadratic residues exactly (p−3)/4 times, and −1 is always a non-residue (window.__paley). FIG no framing; the residues and their differences are enumerated in-browser.
