THE MILANKOVITCH CYCLES

Three slow wobbles in Earth's orbit pace the ice ages: eccentricity (~100 kyr) reshapes the orbit, obliquity (~41 kyr) tilts the axis, and precession (~23 kyr) turns the season of closest approach. They barely change the yearly total sunlight — they move it between seasons and hemispheres. Cool northern summers that fail to melt winter snow are the pacemaker. Rendered, not quoted.

source Milankovitch, M. (1941) Kanon der Erdbestrahlung und seine Anwendung auf das Eiszeitenproblem — Royal Serbian Academy, Special Publication 133, Belgrade (English transl. 1969). AMBER: no stable canonical DOI; cited by author / title / year. catalog record

THE MODEL

The summer insolation anomaly at 65 N is a superposition of three periodic forcings:

I(t) = a⋅cos(2πt/100) + b⋅cos(2πt/41) + c⋅cos(2πt/23)

Eccentricity ~100 kyr (orbit shape) · Obliquity ~41 kyr (axial tilt swings 22.1°–24.5°) · Precession ~23 kyr (the spin axis wobbles). The clocks tick at distinct rates, so the summed forcing carries all three signatures at once.

THE ADVERSARY WALL

"One cycle would do — why insist on three?" Because the paleoclimate record is banded: distinct ~100, ~41 and ~23 kyr power. A single frequency cannot reproduce a three-peaked spectrum. Collapse the periods and the fit to the ice-core / benthic record dies — see window 6.

2

THE GRAVEYARD

The cycles change the total sunlight Earth receives. Fix: obliquity and precession chiefly redistribute insolation between seasons/hemispheres; eccentricity nudges the annual mean by only ~e²/2 (<0.2% at e=0.06).

The dominant beat is the ~100 kyr eccentricity signal, so its forcing is strongest. Fix: eccentricity's direct insolation forcing is the weakest of the three; why the record is 100-kyr paced is still debated. AMBER.

Precession alone dates the glacials. Fix: cool northern-summer insolation (obliquity + precession) is the pacemaker — snow that survives summer. AMBER.
