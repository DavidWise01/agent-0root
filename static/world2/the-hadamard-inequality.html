THE HADAMARD INEQUALITY

a determinant capped by its row lengths
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
Hadamard’s inequality caps how large a determinant can be. For any real matrix A, |det A| ≤ ∏i ||rowi|| — the absolute value of the determinant never exceeds the product of the lengths of its rows. Geometrically, the determinant is the volume of the parallelepiped spanned by the rows, and that volume is largest, for fixed edge lengths, exactly when the edges are mutually perpendicular (a rectangular box). Equality holds if and only if the rows are orthogonal. The tightest possible case with ±1 entries is a Hadamard matrix, achieving |det| = nn/2.

LIT verified live: for thousands of random matrices, |det A| never exceeds ∏||rowi||; orthogonalizing the rows makes it equal; and Sylvester–Hadamard matrices (n = 2, 4, 8) hit the tight bound |det H| = nn/2 (window.__hadamardineq).
