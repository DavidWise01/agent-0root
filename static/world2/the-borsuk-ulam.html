THE BORSUK-ULAM

antipodes that must agree
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
Right now, somewhere on Earth, there are two antipodal points with exactly the same temperature AND the same pressure. Not probably — provably. That is the Borsuk–Ulam theorem (Borsuk 1933, answering Ulam): every continuous map from the n-sphere to ℝⁿ sends some pair of antipodes to the same value. It is the boss theorem of a whole dungeon: ham sandwich, Brouwer’s fixed point, and necklace splitting all fall out of it. The 1D case is an afternoon’s proof: g(θ) = f(θ) − f(θ+π) satisfies g(0) = −g(π), so it must cross zero.

LIT verified live: 200 random continuous circle functions, the antipodal equal-value pair bisected to 10⁻¹⁰ every time (the sign-flip identity checked structurally); and on the sphere, 50 random smooth (temperature, pressure) pairs with the odd map (Δf, Δg) driven below 10⁻⁵ by search-plus-descent — the promised antipodes located (window.__borsukulam). FIG honest boundary: the full theorem for arbitrary continuous maps is cited; instances are executed, and the corollary chain (ham sandwich, Brouwer) is cross-referenced to their own spheres.
