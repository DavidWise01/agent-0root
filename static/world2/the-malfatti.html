THE MALFATTI

the official answer that always loses
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
In 1803 Gian Francesco Malfatti posed the marble problem: cut three circular columns from a triangular prism, wasting the least marble. His answer — three mutually tangent circles, each tangent to two sides — became the textbook construction for a century. It is wrong for every triangle. Goldberg proved (1967) that Malfatti’s configuration is never optimal; Zalgaller & Los proved (1994) the humble greedy procedure — largest circle first, then largest in what remains — always wins. In the equilateral triangle the official answer loses by 1.36%: the shortcut nobody respected beats the answer everybody cited.
