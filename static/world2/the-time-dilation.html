The Model

Two events on one worldline — tick, then tock — are separated by proper time dτ in the clock's own rest frame (same place, so only time differs). In a frame where that clock moves at speed v, the two events are at different places, and the coordinate time between them is

β = v/c   γ = 1/√(1−β²)   dt = γ·dτ

Since γ>1 whenever v>0, the moving clock's dτ is squeezed into a larger ground interval dt: fewer ticks per second of ground time — it runs slow. At v=0, γ=1 and dt=dτ.

The Adversary wall

"If it's all relative, the OTHER frame sees YOUR clock slow too — so who is really slow? The whole thing is a contradiction."

Answer: both are right, and there is no contradiction. Dilation is symmetric because it depends on relative velocity only. The apparent paradox is resolved only by adding a real, frame-independent event — a reunion, which requires acceleration (the-twin-paradox). Symmetry of the effect ≠ symmetry of the histories.

2
The Graveyard
  • "Time dilation makes the moving clock run fast." No — slow. dt=γdτ with γ>1 means ground time dt exceeds the clock's own dτ: fewer ticks per ground second.
  • "Only clocks are affected; real physical decay isn't." Muon lifetime is a clock. Their measured survival to sea level is the effect, tested daily.
  • "The effect scales with v, so +v and −v differ." It depends on β² — identical for +v and −v. Direction is irrelevant.
