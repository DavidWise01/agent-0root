THE VORTICITY

Vorticity is the local spin of a fluid — how fast a tiny paddle wheel set adrift would turn. The engine computes ω = curl v straight from the velocity field, and lands on the surprise: a free vortex has circular streamlines yet no spin at all off its axis. Curved motion and local rotation are two different things. Rendered, not quoted.

THE MODEL

In 2D, vorticity is the scalar curl of the velocity field:

ω = ∂vy/∂x − ∂vx/∂y

Three canonical fields test it:

rigid rotation v = Ω×r = (−Ωy, Ωx) → uniform ω = 2Ω.

simple shear v = (y, 0) → ω = −1.

free vortex vθ = Γ/(2πr) → ω = 0 for every point off the axis.

Partials are taken by central finite difference; linear fields come out exact, the vortex leaves a residual bounded below.

THE ADVERSARY WALL

“Curved streamlines mean the fluid is rotating. The free vortex whirls in circles — obviously it spins.”

Wrong, and it is the whole point. Circulation around a loop can be non-zero while the vorticity inside is zero, because all the spin is concentrated on the singular axis. A paddle wheel dropped off-axis orbits the center without turning on its own axle. Curvature of a path ≠ rotation of an element.

2

THE GRAVEYARD

“Vorticity equals the angular velocity of the fluid.”

→ It is twice the local angular velocity: ω = 2Ω. Off by a factor of 2 forever.

“Irrotational just means the fluid is at rest or moving in straight lines.”

→ The free vortex is irrotational yet everywhere in motion on circular streamlines. Irrotational is about local spin, not global path.

“Divergence and curl both measure how a field turns.”

→ Divergence measures spreading; curl measures rotation. Confusing them is exactly the tamper below.

6

THE TAMPER

The disclosed planted void: replace curl v with div v in the “vorticity” operator. A rigid rotation has div v = 0, so it would be reported as having zero spin — the ω = 2Ω invariant breaks and the Witness (7) catches it live.
