THE LIOUVILLE NUMBER

a number approximated absurdly well by rationals
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
Liouville’s number L = ∑k≥1 10-k! = 0.110001000000000000000001… (a 1 at every factorial position, 0 elsewhere) was the first number ever proven transcendental (Liouville, 1844). The trick: its digits leave enormous runs of zeros, so the truncations pn/qn approximate L absurdly well — |L - pn/qn| < 1/qnn for every n. But Liouville proved an algebraic number of degree d can never be approximated better than c/qd. Since L can be approximated to any power, it is not algebraic of any degree — it is transcendental.

LIT verified live with exact big-integer arithmetic: for the truncations of L = ∑10-k!, the approximation error |L - pn/qn| is strictly less than 1/qnn for n = 1..5, and the approximation exponent (n+1) grows without bound — beating any fixed algebraic degree (window.__liouvillenumber).
