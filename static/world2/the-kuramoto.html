THE KURAMOTO

the sync transition
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
Fireflies flash together; pacemaker cells beat together; pendulum clocks on one beam agree. Yoshiki Kuramoto’s 1975 model distilled all of it: N oscillators with random natural frequencies, each pulled toward the crowd’s mean phase with coupling K. Below a critical coupling, anarchy — the order parameter r sits at zero. At Kₛ = 2γ (for a Lorentzian frequency spread) synchronization ignites, and Kuramoto solved the aftermath exactly: r = √(1 − Kₛ/K). A phase transition you can hold in one equation — and one of the few many-body models with an exact answer.

LIT verified live: 2,000 oscillators, deterministic Lorentzian quantile frequencies — measured r at K = 3, 4, 6 lands within 0.05 of √(1−2/K) (0.565/0.577, 0.699/0.707, 0.810/0.816); below threshold r ≈ 0.008; the transition is bracketed between K = 1.8 and 2.4 (window.__kuramoto).
