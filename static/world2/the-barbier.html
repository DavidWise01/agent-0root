THE BARBIER

every constant-width curve has the same perimeter
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
Barbier’s theorem says every curve of constant width w has exactly the same perimeter: πw — identical to a circle of diameter w, no matter how un-circular the curve is. A curve has constant width if, squeezed between two parallel lines from any direction, the gap is always w (so it rolls smoothly under a board, like a circle). The Reuleaux triangle — three circular arcs on an equilateral triangle — is the pointiest example, yet its perimeter is still πw. Barbier proved this holds for all of them: constant width alone forces the perimeter, independent of shape.

LIT verified live: Reuleaux polygons (triangle, pentagon, heptagon) are built as arcs; measuring the width in 180 directions confirms it is constant, and summing the boundary arc-length gives πw to ~1e-3, for every one of them (window.__barbier). FIG no framing; the width sampling and the perimeter integration both run in-browser and confirm perimeter = πw regardless of shape.
