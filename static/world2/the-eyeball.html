THE EYEBALL

the equal gaze
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
Two circles regard each other across a distance d. From the center of each, draw the two tangent lines to the OTHER circle — the ‘gaze’. Each gaze cuts a chord out of the gazer’s own circle (the ‘pupil’). The eyeball theorem: the two pupils are always equal — both have length 2r₁r₂/d — no matter how mismatched the circles. A big eye and a small eye, gazing at each other, contract to identical pupils: the formula is symmetric in r₁, r₂ and neither eye can tell which is which from the pupil alone.

LIT verified live: 200 random circle pairs — both chords constructed explicitly from the tangent geometry, equal to each other and to 2r₁r₂/d to 10⁻¹² (worst ~10⁻¹⁶); the independent route re-derives the tangent line, confirms its distance to the far center is exactly r₂, and re-measures the chord (window.__eyeball). FIG the theorem’s origin is obscure — a folklore gem circulating through modern problem collections (Gutierrez’s Go Geometry among them); we verify the mathematics directly rather than assert a paternity.
