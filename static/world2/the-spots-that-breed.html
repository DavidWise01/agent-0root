THE SPOTS THAT BREED

Turing's morphogenesis — how a leopard gets its spots
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
Gray–Scott reaction–diffusion. Two chemicals, U and V, spread across a surface (diffusion) and react (V turns U into more V; U is fed in; V decays). From these purely local rules — Alan Turing’s last big idea, morphogenesis — global patterns appear on their own: spots, stripes, mazes, and in one famous regime, blobs that grow and split like dividing cells. It is how a leopard gets its spots and a zebra its stripes, from nothing but two numbers diffusing.

LIT verified: from a near-uniform seed, spatial structure emerges (the flat state is unstable — a Turing instability), the concentrations stay bounded (no blow-up), and different feed/kill constants produce distinct pattern classes (measured by very different final textures). In the mitosis regime the spots visibly divide (shown live). FIG ‘spots that breed’ is the picture; the reaction-diffusion dynamics, the instability, and the boundedness are exact.
