What changed: the corpus. Same stack, same eight heads, same 13-token window. Swapping 1,080 tokens of my own summary prose for 184,925 tokens of eve.txt took held-out perplexity from 10.34 to 5.274 and accuracy from 33.2% to 48.3%, and it now beats a trigram table (5.619) instead of losing to one. Train perplexity 5.058 against test 5.274 — the memorisation that plagued the small run is gone.

Two things did the work, and only one was data. The readout is now a 128-unit ReLU layer rather than a linear map. On the earlier corpus that change alone was worth roughly two nats. I had spent four turns blaming the corpus for a ceiling that was partly my own architecture, and the scaling curve is what exposed it.

A 4-gram table still wins at 3.855. Pure memorisation remains the thing to beat at this scale, and the crossover where attention's context reach pays for its sample inefficiency is still far above 185k tokens.

And the corpus is itself model output. eve.txt is roughly 99.6% Gemini — 714 characters of your framing, then 184,198 of generated text. Measured against human-written prose at matched size it carries lower character entropy (4.070 vs 4.158), lower conditional entropy (3.305 vs 3.478), 26% fewer word types, and a Heaps exponent of 0.554 against 0.746. It is easier to model because there is less in it.
