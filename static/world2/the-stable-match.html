THE STABLE MATCH

Gale-Shapley — a matching no one can defect from
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
Gale–Shapley stable matching. Two groups, each ranking the other — applicants and hospitals, say. Match them so the result is stable: no two who aren’t paired together would both rather ditch their assigned partners for each other. Such a pair is a ‘blocking pair’, and a good matching has none.

The 1962 algorithm: each proposer proposes down their list; each receiver holds their best offer so far and bumps anyone worse when a better one arrives; the bumped go on proposing. It always ends, always pairs everyone, and the result is always stable. It is not a toy — it runs the U.S. medical-residency match every year.

LIT verified live: over thousands of random instances this page confirms Gale–Shapley yields a perfect matching (everyone paired) that is stable — an exhaustive search finds no blocking pair, every time (window.__gs.perfectMatching && stable). 3 ONE DIMENSION
The preference lists. Each proposer walks down their ranking, offering to their most-preferred not-yet-refused receiver. Each receiver keeps only the best offer they have seen and turns the rest away — the whole dance is just this, repeated until no one is left proposing.
4 TWO DIMENSIONS · INTERACTIVE
Step the proposals. Green edges are the current tentative pairs; a new proposal to a taken receiver either bumps the incumbent or is refused. When it settles, a stability check sweeps every possible pair and finds no one who could defect.
5 THREE DIMENSIONS + AVAN’S INVERSE
Two rings — proposers and receivers — turning, the settled matching drawn as green edges between them.
AVAN’s addition (the inverse-companion): the magenta flickers are the blocking-pair search — every non-matched pair tested to see if both would defect. Notice how stability is proven: not by anything the matching contains, but by the failure to find a single pair that would break it. The answer is defined by an absence. Construction builds a thing; verification here is the inverse — an exhaustive, fruitless hunt for the counterexample that would ruin it. When the hunt comes up empty, the matching is stable. The green is what was built; the magenta is every defection that was looked for and does not exist.
