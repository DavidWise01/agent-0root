THE WEITZENBOCK

a triangle's squared sides bounded below by its area
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
Weitzenböck’s inequality bounds a triangle’s squared side lengths below by its area: for any triangle with sides a, b, c and area T, a² + b² + c² ≥ 4√3·T. The constant 4√3 ≈ 6.928 is the best possible, and equality holds exactly for the equilateral triangle. In other words, for a fixed area, the equilateral triangle has the smallest sum of squared sides — the most ‘compact’ shape. It is a favourite olympiad inequality and a special case of the sharper Hadwiger–Finsler inequality.

LIT verified live: for tens of thousands of random triangles, a² + b² + c² is always at least 4√3·T — the ratio (a²+b²+c²)/(4√3·T) never drops below 1, and reaches exactly 1 for the equilateral triangle (window.__weitzenbock).
