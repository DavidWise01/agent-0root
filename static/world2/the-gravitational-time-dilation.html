THE GRAVITATIONAL TIME DILATION

A clock deeper in a gravity well ticks slower — not by illusion, by the metric itself. The factor is Schwarzschild's dτ/dt = √(1 − rs/r), with rs = 2GM/c². It is why a GPS satellite clock, higher in the well, runs fast by tens of microseconds a day and must be corrected. Down the center, data flows: mass and depth go in, the metric decides, the tick-rate comes out. The blue team builds and defends it; the red team tries to break it.

THE MODEL — the Schwarzschild factor

Around a spherical mass M, proper time τ (a real clock) and coordinate time t (a clock at infinity) are locked by one exact factor:

dτ/dt = √(1 − rs/r),   rs = 2GM/c².

It is < 1 everywhere outside the horizon: deeper (smaller r) is always slower. It → 1 far away (r >> rs, no dilation) and → 0 at r = rs (frozen, to the distant observer). For the current body:

THE LINEAGE — deeper wells run slow AVAN

Einstein 1916: mass curves spacetime; the same curvature that bends orbits also stretches the tick between clocks. The one factor √(1 − rs/r) is the hinge of a whole family.

At r = rs it hits 0 — that surface is the-schwarzschild-radius, where escape velocity = c. Its inverse, 1/√(1−rs/r) − 1, is the redshift the-gravitational-redshift measures. And its weak-field slope is the correction GPS lives on. Each sphere is the next one's premise.

THE ADVERSARY

WALL The Schwarzschild metric is idealized: one non-rotating, uncharged, spherical mass in vacuum. Real Earth is oblate and spinning — the true exterior is Kerr, with frame-dragging. GPS also needs the special-relativistic velocity term (−7.2 µs/day) and the geoid; the net is +38, not +45.7.

And "dτ/dt" is a ratio to a coordinate clock at infinity, not something you can hold. What two real observers compare is the ratio of their factors — a common mistake is to read the bare number as an absolute rate. The metric is not the whole of gravity; it is the first proof that time has a computable depth.

THE GRAVEYARD

"Time slows because acceleration/g is stronger down there." Cut. It tracks the potential Φ, not local g. A clock in a deep but flat-potential region (zero local acceleration) still runs slow.

"GPS clocks tick slow, being in orbit." Cut. Gravitationally they tick fast (+45.7 µs/day); even after the −7.2 µs velocity term the net is +38 µs/day fast.

"At the horizon, time literally stops." Kept, corrected. Only in the distant observer's coordinate time. The infalling clock ticks normally straight through r = rs.

THE TAMPER — break it

The red team's move: flip the sign under the root — use √(1 + rs/r) so clocks deeper in the well run faster. Slip that past and GPS "corrections" go the wrong way. The blue team's witness (window 7) is watching.

Flip the sign and the deeper-is-slower law inverts — the witness recomputes, finds a deeper clock running fast, and turns red. Nothing is faked; the attack is real and it is caught.
