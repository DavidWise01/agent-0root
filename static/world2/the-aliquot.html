THE ALIQUOT

the number that equals the sum of its parts
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
The aliquot sum s(n) adds up all of a number’s proper divisors (every divisor except itself). It sorts the integers into three ancient classes: deficient (s<n), abundant (s>n), and the rare perfect (s = n): 6 = 1+2+3, 28 = 1+2+4+7+14. Two numbers form an amicable pair when each is the aliquot sum of the other — 220 and 284, known since antiquity.

LIT verified live: exhaustively to 10000, the only perfect numbers are 6, 28, 496, 8128, and the amicable pairs include 220&284 — each confirmed by summing divisors (window.__aliquot). FIG no framing; exact integer divisor sums.
