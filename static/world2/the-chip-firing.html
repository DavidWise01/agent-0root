THE CHIP-FIRING

avalanches that forget their order
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
Put chips on a grid. Any cell holding 4 or more fires: sends one chip to each neighbor. Fires can trigger fires — avalanches. The abelian sandpile theorem (Dhar 1990): no matter what order you fire in, the final stable configuration is identical — chaos with a deterministic destination. Stranger: the stable configurations form a group, and its identity element — the configuration that changes nothing when sandpile-added — is a breathtaking fractal, a symmetric mandala that nobody designed. This is also the birthplace of self-organized criticality (Bak–Tang–Wiesenfeld 1987): the sandpile drives itself to the critical point where avalanches of every size occur.

LIT verified live on a 25×25 grid: the same random configuration stabilized under 20 different random firing orders — byte-identical results every time; the identity element computed by the classic recipe e = stab(2m − stab(2m)), verified idempotent (e ⊕ e = e); and certified recurrent by Dhar’s burning test — the boundary wave burns all 625 sites exactly once (window.__chipfiring).
