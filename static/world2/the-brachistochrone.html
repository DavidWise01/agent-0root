the dip that arrives first
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
June 1696: Johann Bernoulli challenges ‘the sharpest mathematicians in the world’: down which curve does a bead slide between two points in the least time? Not the straight line. The answer is the brachistochrone — an inverted cycloid, which dives steeply to build speed and spends it on the flat. Newton received the problem after a day at the Mint and solved it overnight, publishing anonymously; Bernoulli saw through it instantly: ‘I recognize the lion by his claw.’ The challenge founded the calculus of variations — and the same cycloid is the tautochrone, its sister sphere.
