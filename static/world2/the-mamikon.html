THE MAMIKON

an annulus worth only its tangent length
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
Mamikon’s annulus is the front door of ‘visual calculus’. Draw a ring between two concentric circles, and let ℓ be the half-length of a chord of the outer circle that just grazes the inner one. Then the ring’s area is πℓ² — and the radii themselves have vanished: a skinny ring around a planet and a fat ring around a coin have the same area if their tangent half-chords match. Mamikon Mnatsakanian’s 1959 insight (later developed with Tom Apostol): sweep the tangent segment around the ring, then translate every segment to a common point — the ‘tangent cluster’ forms a plain disk of radius ℓ, with no integral in sight. The same idea dispatches the cycloid area and a family of classical results.
