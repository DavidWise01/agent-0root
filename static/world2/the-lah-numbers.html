THE LAH NUMBERS

numbers linking the rising and falling factorials
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
The Lah numbers L(n,k) are the exact exchange rate between the two natural kinds of factorial. The rising factorial x(n) = x(x+1)…(x+n-1) and the falling factorial (x)k = x(x-1)…(x-k+1) each build a ‘staircase’ product, one climbing and one descending. The unsigned Lah numbers convert one into the other: x(n) = ∑k L(n,k) (x)k, with the clean closed form L(n,k) = C(n-1, k-1) · n!/k!. Combinatorially, L(n,k) counts the ways to split n labelled items into k non-empty ordered lists. They sit between the Stirling numbers as the ‘both-ordered’ case, and satisfy L(n,1) = n!, L(n,n) = 1.

LIT verified live: L(n,k) = C(n-1,k-1)·n!/k! is an integer with L(n,1) = n! and L(n,n) = 1, and the identity x(n) = ∑k L(n,k)(x)k holds exactly for a range of x and n (window.__lah). FIG no framing; the Lah closed form and the factorial identity are computed independently in-browser.
