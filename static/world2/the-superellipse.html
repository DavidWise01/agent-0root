THE SUPERELLIPSE

between the circle and the square
1 WHAT IT IS · WHAT IT DOES · FACT OR FICTION
Between the circle and the square, said Piet Hein, runs beauty. His superellipse |x/a|ⁿ + |y/b|ⁿ = 1 interpolates them: n = 2 is the ellipse, n → ∞ the rectangle, and n = 2.5 — his choice for Sergels Torg in Stockholm (1959), after architects deadlocked between round and rectangular — became a design icon (tables, stadiums, and the ‘squircle’ n = 4 of modern UI corners). The area has an exact closed form through the Gamma function: A = 4ab·Γ(1+1/n)²/Γ(1+2/n).

LIT verified live: the Gamma formula (via a Lanczos implementation built in-page) against direct numeric integration at n = 2, 2.5, 4, 8 — agreement to 10⁻⁴ — with the n = 2 anchor landing on π to nine decimals and n = 50 reaching 3.9974 → 4, the square in the limit (window.__superellipse).
