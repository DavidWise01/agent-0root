THE FRESNEL EQUATIONS

Exactly how much light a surface throws back and how much it lets through — split by polarization, angle by angle. Two amplitude laws set the whole budget: R + T = 1, a faint 4% off plain glass at head-on, and one angle where p-polarized reflection vanishes to nothing. Rendered, not quoted.

The Model

Amplitude in, energy out

Light of amplitude 1 hits the boundary between index n₁ and n₂ at incidence θᵢ. Snell fixes the refracted angle: n₁ sinθᵢ = n₂ sinθₜ. Fresnel gives the reflected amplitude for each polarization:

rs = (n1·cosθi − n2·cosθt)/(n1·cosθi + n2·cosθt) rp = (n2·cosθi − n1·cosθt)/(n2·cosθi + n1·cosθt) Rs = rs² Rp = rp²

Reflectance is amplitude squared. Transmittance carries a geometric weight — power spreads across the refracted beam:

T = (n2·cosθt)/(n1·cosθi) · t² t_s = 2n1·cosθi/(n1·cosθi+n2·cosθt) t_p = 2n1·cosθi/(n2·cosθi+n1·cosθt)

That weight is the load-bearing part. Without it, energy does not close.

The Lineage

The budget behind the coating

This is the amplitude law under every neighbour. the-brewster-angle is one point on the p-curve — the zero. the-thin-lens loses 4% per glass face to exactly this reflection; anti-reflection coatings are engineered to cancel Rs, Rp at one wavelength. Same equations feed the-fabry-perot, whose finesse π√R/(1−R) is built from this very R.

One index step → the whole reflected/transmitted split. Fresnel, 1823.

Red Team · attacks & breaks
1
The Adversary

WALL  Where it really cracks

Metals & absorbers. These pure real-index equations assume a lossless dielectric. A metal has complex n = n − ik; R never reaches 0 and Brewster becomes a shallow "pseudo-Brewster" minimum. Real-only math is wrong for gold, silver, silicon-at-UV.

Total internal reflection. Going glass→air past the critical angle, cosθt goes imaginary — Math.sqrt(1−s²) returns NaN. R = 1 but the phase (evanescent wave) is lost. This engine is scoped n₁<n₂, no TIR.

Absorption & roughness. R+T=1 only if nothing is absorbed or scattered. Real surfaces bury a few % in loss.

2
The Graveyard

Struck claims

"Glass reflects the same fraction at every angle."
→ No. 4% only at head-on; Rₛ climbs to 100% at grazing.

"Reflected light is never polarized."
→ At Brewster the reflection is purely s-polarized — R_p = 0. That is polarized sunglasses' whole trick.

"T is just 1 − r²."
→ T needs the (n₂cosθₜ)/(n₁cosθᵢ) weight. Drop it and energy overshoots or undershoots 1.

The planted void
