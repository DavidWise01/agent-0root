Logic — the algebra of thought. The lineage that walked logic into mathematics: from Thales of Miletus' first deductive proof (c. 600 BCE — a truth that must be PROVEN, not merely seen), through Aristotle's syllogism, Euclid's axioms and the Stoics' propositions, to Leibniz's dream of calculation and George Boole's algebra of true and false — the direct ancestor of every gate in PSĒPHOS. One logician per sphere, each with a live demonstration of their idea, and each honest about how sure we really are. (λογική, logikē — 'of reasoning', from logos: word, ratio, account.)
